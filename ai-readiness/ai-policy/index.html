What should a small business AI acceptable-use policy cover?

A small business AI acceptable-use policy should say which AI tools are approved, what data may never go into them, who reviews AI output before it reaches a client, who owns the policy and how often it is reviewed. For most teams it fits in a few pages and should be written so every employee can follow it.

Do we need an AI policy if we are a 20-person company?

Yes, if anyone on your team uses AI tools with client or company information. Most teams already do, often on personal accounts. A small business does not need a committee, just a short policy, an approved-tool list, data rules and one person who owns it.

What to include

  • Purpose and scope: who the policy covers, including contractors
  • Approved tools: which AI tools are allowed, on business accounts only
  • Data rules: what may never be entered (client data, financial records, employee information, passwords)
  • Review: which AI output a person must check before it goes to a client or into the books
  • Disclosure: when to tell a client that AI helped with the work
  • Ownership: who owns the policy, who approves new tools and who staff ask when unsure
  • Training: how new hires learn the policy, with real examples from your business
  • Review schedule: quarterly or twice a year, because the tools change fast

How do you stop staff pasting client data into ChatGPT?

Give them a better option and a clear rule. Move the team onto approved business accounts with data controls turned on, write down which data may never be entered, show real examples in training, and name one person they can ask. Rules without an approved tool tend to be ignored.

How to roll it out

  • List the AI tools your team uses today, including free and personal accounts
  • Decide which tools are approved and set up business accounts
  • Write the policy in plain language, a few pages long
  • Train the team with examples from their own work
  • Name the owner and put the first review on the calendar

Frequently asked questions

How long should a small business AI policy be?

A few pages. Long enough to cover approved tools, data rules, review and ownership, short enough that every employee reads it.

Can we use a free AI policy template?

A template is a fine starting point. The work is in tailoring it: listing the tools your team actually uses, the data your business actually holds and who actually reviews AI output.

Who should own the AI policy in a small business?

One named person, usually an operations lead or the owner, who approves new tools, answers staff questions and runs the review.

How often should we update it?

At least twice a year, and whenever the team starts using a new AI tool or a new kind of data.
